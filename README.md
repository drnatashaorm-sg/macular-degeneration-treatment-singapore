Macular degeneration is one of the leading causes of vision loss among older adults, yet its early symptoms are often subtle enough to be overlooked or mistaken for normal ageing changes. Recognising the early warning signs of macular degeneration Singapore patients commonly experience can make a significant difference in preserving vision, since earlier detection generally allows for more effective management of the condition.

**What Is Macular Degeneration?**

Macular degeneration affects the macula, the small central part of the retina responsible for sharp, detailed central vision, the kind needed for reading, recognising faces and driving. While peripheral vision generally remains unaffected, central vision can become increasingly blurred, distorted or, in more advanced cases, develop blind spots.
The condition typically falls into two main categories, dry macular degeneration, which progresses gradually, and wet macular degeneration, which can develop more rapidly and cause more significant vision loss if not addressed promptly.

**Early Warning Signs to Watch For**

Because macular degeneration primarily affects central vision, many of its early signs relate specifically to tasks that rely heavily on sharp, detailed sight.
Blurred or hazy central vision, particularly when reading small print or trying to focus on fine details, is often one of the earliest noticeable symptoms.

Straight lines appearing wavy or distorted, a symptom often first noticed when looking at door frames, window blinds or lined paper.
Difficulty recognising faces, especially from a distance, even though peripheral vision remains largely intact.
A blank or blurry spot in your central vision, which may be subtle at first but can gradually become more noticeable over time.
Colours appear less vibrant or more difficult to distinguish, particularly in low light conditions.
Needing brighter light for reading or close up tasks that previously did not require additional lighting.

**Why Early Detection Matters So Much**

Unlike some other eye conditions, macular degeneration cannot currently be cured, which makes early detection and appropriate management particularly important for slowing progression and preserving as much central vision as possible.
Early stage dry macular degeneration often progresses slowly, and lifestyle adjustments alongside regular monitoring can help manage its impact over time.

Wet macular degeneration, while less common, can progress rapidly and cause more significant vision loss if left untreated, making prompt evaluation especially important if symptoms appear suddenly.
Regular monitoring allows your eye specialist to track any changes over time and adjust management strategies as needed.
Catching the condition early gives patients more time to adapt daily habits and explore supportive strategies before central vision is significantly affected.

**Risk Factors to Be Aware Of**

Understanding what increases your risk of developing macular degeneration can help guide decisions about screening frequency and lifestyle habits.

Age remains the most significant risk factor, with the condition becoming increasingly common after the age of 60.
A family history of macular degeneration can meaningfully increase your own risk of developing the condition.
Smoking has been strongly linked to a higher risk of macular degeneration and can also accelerate its progression.
Certain lifestyle factors, including diet and cardiovascular health, may also play a role in overall risk.
